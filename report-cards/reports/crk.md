# So you want to work on nēhiyawēwin?

This report is designed to help compile existing resources to give you an idea of where your efforts can be best spent.

**ISO 639-3:** `crk` · macrolanguage `cre`

**Language family:** **Algic** › Algonquian-Blackfoot › Algonquian › … › Plains Creeic ([Glottolog](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/plai1258))

**Endangerment:** 🔴 moribund (level 4 of 6 on Glottolog’s scale; source: Glottolog)

**Speakers:** about 13,000 first- and second-language speakers ([CLDR](https://www.unicode.org/cldr/charts/latest/supplemental/territory_language_information.html)) · 34,000 first-language ([Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q56699#P1098), 1982)

**Official / recognised in:** Canada

**Status:** Regional official language

### How good are we at identifying data?

Language identification (LangID or LID) is a classification task that identifies what language a text is in and it remains a challenging task for many languages. We consider a set of 10 popular models and test on samples of 7 common eval datasets. We ask which models can even predict this language (i.e. how many of them have the language code as one of their labels), whether the language is represented in any or all of the eval datasets, and how well the models do.

- **2 of 10** LangID models evaluated have this language code as a label that can be predicted: GlotLID, funlangid.
- **Eval datasets (2):** Tatoeba (n=54), Wikipedia (n=90)
- **Best model:** GlotLID — F1 0.711 (P 0.985, R 0.557, FPR 1.38e-05) averaged over 2 dataset(s). Runner-up: funlangid (F1 0.059).

### What can you do?

- To help make more data appear in Common Crawl, you can help **seed the crawl:** add URLs of sites written in nēhiyawēwin to [commoncrawl/web-languages](https://github.com/commoncrawl/web-languages).
- **Create LangID data** **(priority: current LangID is weak)**: label text from nēhiyawēwin *and* from closely related languages that might be confused with it.
- **Gather data from new sources:**
  - Look for datasets built for other purposes (UD treebanks, ASR transcripts, etc.) that contain high-quality text reliably in nēhiyawēwin and compile them ready for training data.
  - Find books or other materials that can be digitised, OCRed, or otherwise made available (check the license!).
  - Make them available on Hugging Face and tag them for the language.
- **Create structured data:** nēhiyawēwin has no UD treebank or wordnet yet. You could start a [Universal Dependencies treebank](https://universaldependencies.org/contributing/); build or link a wordnet via the [Open Multilingual Wordnet](https://omwn.org/).
- **Record a speaker:** there's no [Wikitongues](https://wikitongues.org/languages/crk/) video of nēhiyawēwin yet. Openly licensed recordings help speech work and show what the language sounds like.

## What evals are available?

We analyzed categories of popular (base) language model evaluations:

  1. **Linguistic knowledge**. Ability to discriminate grammatical from ungrammatical sentences via minimal pairs, e.g. BLiMP and MultiBLiMP, and cloze-style word prediction, e.g. LAMBADA.
  2. **Pragmatics, discourse, and co-reference**. Understanding meaning beyond the literal sentence: pronoun resolution and Winograd-style coreference (e.g. WSC, WinoGrande, XWinograd), discourse coherence, figurative language and idioms, implicature, negation and presupposition.
  3. **Commonsense reasoning**. These tasks evaluate a combination of language understanding and world knowledge, e.g. HellaSwag-, PIQA-, COPA- and StoryCloze-style datasets.
  4. **Reading comprehension**. Answering questions about a given passage, e.g. Belebele.
  5. **Knowledge and open-domain QA**. World knowledge, e.g. MMLU.

We also consider available machine translation benchmarks, which can also be used for parallel NLL evaluations among other things.

We ordered these roughly in level of complexity and plot them in a pyramid shape, where we expect that the lower parts of the pyramid are filled in before the higher parts.

The scope of this analysis did not include classification tasks or tasks designed for bidirectional models, nor does it include post-trained model evals (e.g. instruction following) or tasks about reasoning, coding, and domain-specific material, e.g. medical or legal topics.

**1** benchmark datasets in the survey include nēhiyawēwin.

### What tasks are missing?
- **1. Linguistic knowledge:** BLiMP, LAMBADA
- **2. Pragmatics, discourse & coreference:** Coherence & discourse, Figurative language & idioms, Gender-bias diagnostic (Winograd-format), Implicature & indirect answers, Negation, Presupposition & scalar implicature, Pronoun/coref (WSC-style), WNLI, WSC, WinoGrande
- **3. Commonsense reasoning:** COPA, HellaSwag, PIQA, Physical commonsense, Social commonsense, StoryCloze, Temporal reasoning, World knowledge
- **4. Reading comprehension:** Multiple-choice reading comprehension (Belebele-style), Extractive reading comprehension (SQuAD-style)
- **5. Knowledge & open-domain QA:** Knowledge/exam MCQ (MMLU-style), Open-domain / closed-book QA

### Community benchmarks you can contribute to

- **[Global PIQA](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mrlbenchmarks/global-piqa-nonparallel)** (❌ not yet — contribute it!): Physical-commonsense questions written by native speakers about their own culture.
- **[Last Translation Benchmark](https://github.com/zouharvi/last-translation-benchmark)** (❌ not yet — contribute it!): Live benchmark of hard-to-translate inputs: submit a source, reference translation and verification rules that current MT systems fail; 10 accepted examples earns co-authorship.
- **[Cultural Riddles](https://culturalriddles.ai/)** (❌ not yet — contribute it!): Human-written, culturally grounded riddles (Cohere Labs Community); open contribution process.

### What can you do?

- **Implement tasks on the [LM Evaluation Harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)** for the existing nēhiyawēwin datasets listed above, if they're not there already.
- **Make a leaderboard.** What is the best available model for nēhiyawēwin? Does it make sense to train one?
  - There is too little measured data (no FineWeb/Goldfish English-equivalent size) to say; data collection comes first.
- **Create new evals.** Start from the bottom of the pyramid. Since the largest tagged (multilingual) model is 1.9T, aim no higher than
  *5. Knowledge & open-domain QA* for now; the first gap to fill is **1. Linguistic knowledge**.
  (If the only model is ~100M parameters, don't build QA tasks: the models probably can't do them.)
- **Translate existing evals, and contribute to the community benchmarks above** (Global PIQA, Last Translation Benchmark, Cultural Riddles).
